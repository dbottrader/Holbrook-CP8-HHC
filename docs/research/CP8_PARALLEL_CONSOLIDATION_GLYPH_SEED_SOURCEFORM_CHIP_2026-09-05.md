## 6. Sacred geometry ↔ semiconductor topology branch

### 6.1 Established semiconductor geometry

Modern IC layout is literally a geometric implementation: polygons on process layers specify conducting, semiconducting, insulating, transistor, via, and interconnect regions. Cells are instantiated hierarchically and repeatedly across a chip.

NVIDIA Blackwell supplies a concrete current example: two reticle-sized dies operate as one GPU; Blackwell Ultra organizes up to 160 SMs into GPCs; each SM contains repeated CUDA/Tensor/other compute structures. RTX Blackwell documentation similarly describes GPC → TPC → SM hierarchy.

### 6.2 Structural comparison vector

Freeze a substrate-neutral feature vector for both chip layouts and sacred-geometry constructions:

`V = [symmetry_order, repetition_rate, hierarchy_depth, packing_density, node_degree_distribution, path_length_distribution, centrality_profile, scale_ratio_distribution, modularity, boundary_to_core_ratio, loop_density, redundancy]`

Controls must include unrelated engineered layouts, random planar graphs, ordinary decorative geometry, crystal lattices, and non-sacred tilings.

### 6.3 Current finding

SUPPORTED: both semiconductor layout and sacred/geometric constructions can instantiate symmetry, repetition, hierarchy, packing, modularity, networks, and constrained spatial relations.

NOT ESTABLISHED: NVIDIA/TSMC or another chip vendor intentionally implemented sacred-geometry doctrine.

TESTABLE HYPOTHESIS: particular sacred-geometry families and high-performance chip floorplans may share nontrivial graph/topological invariants beyond what generic constrained planar/hierarchical systems produce. This requires actual floorplan geometry and controls; visual resemblance is insufficient.
